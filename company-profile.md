## 2. Culture and values

- **Patriarchal founding culture.** The founder built the company about 50 years ago, and his imprint is still strong.
- **People first, profit second.** This sits at the heart of the company. The foundation expects long-term growth, local jobs in the region and no short-term cuts.
- **Strict values, no quarterly pressure.** The company has no quarterly results to defend, but its values are not negotiable. CEO: "Our patients and pharmacists must never pay for our speed."
- **New tone: make money, with a mission.** Management wants a conquering spirit backed by a strong mission. This new tone sits on top of the people-first heritage.

## 3. Financial situation

- **Low capacity to invest.** Because the foundation owns the company, there is little cash to invest. Every euro spent has to pay back clearly.
- **Growth gap.** The company grows at about **half the sector's rate**. The target is to grow at **1.5× the sector's rate**, which means tripling its relative growth.
- **Working capital is high.** Finished goods wait in quarantine until quality releases them: about €20M at cost on any day at the Gascony plant alone.

## 4. Governance

## 6. Strategic mandate: why they called us

- **The supply chain does not deliver.** Pharmacists, who are the channel selling the dermo-cosmetics, face **stockouts**. These are frequent at export distributors during launches.
- **Time to market is too slow:** 15 to 20 months, against about **4 months for the "neo-pharma" players**. Big beauty groups are also moving into the pharmacy channel.
- **International growth** (Asia, the Americas) needs reliable supply and fast launches.
- **GMP constraint.** Under good manufacturing practice rules, every change is slow and must be documented. Any computerized system that affects quality must be validated before use.

## 7. What was sold (pre-sales)

The pharmaceutical operations director signed for **12 weeks at the dermo-cosmetics plant in Gascony**, with 4 FDEs.

> "Cut the time from end of packaging to batch release from 18 days to 10. An AI assistant reviews batch records, flags anomalies and prepares deviation investigations. In the target state, conforming batches are released automatically."

### Gascony plant: key figures

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Batches a year | About 2,400, so about 10 per working day |
| End of packaging to release | 18 days on average; 6 days when nothing goes wrong |
| Batches with a deviation | About 1 in 5 |
| Time to close a deviation | 12 days on average |
| Batch records | About 40% on paper, scanned at the end; the rest electronic |
| Lab results | In the LIMS |
| Finished goods awaiting release | About €20M at cost |
| Reviewers | 15, working most Saturdays |

## 8. First FDE reading: what pre-sales got wrong

1. **"Released automatically" collides with the law and with the QP.** Only the qualified person can certify a batch. The AI proposes and the QP decides. She was not in pre-sales, so the scope has to be re-signed with her.
2. **The €9M is a one-time cash release, not EBIT.** €20M ÷ 18 days ≈ €1.1M per day of release time. 8 days ≈ **€9M of cash, released once**. The recurring P&L gain is only the carrying cost, roughly €1M a year at an assumed ~10% rate (*to agree with the CFO*). The CFO has already told the board otherwise, so tell him right away.
3. **Deviations alone do not explain 18 days.** 80% × 6 days + 20% × (6 + 12) days ≈ **8.4 days**. The remaining ~10 days are probably queueing: reviewer capacity, paper records scanned at the end, waiting for the QP. **Find the real bottleneck before building anything.**
4. **Validation is a hidden cost and an owner question.** It falls on the CIO's team, which is already the bottleneck. Budget it in the plan, given that the company has little cash to invest.
5. **Mandate vs scope.** The real pain is stockouts and a 15-to-20-month time to market. Batch release is one lever, so link it explicitly to on-time launches and service level, not only to inventory.
6. **People.** The reviewers' freed capacity goes first to removing the Saturdays, not to cutting jobs. This is consistent with people-first values and with what the foundation expects.

## 9. Open questions for kickoff

- Does the QP agree to an assistant that prepares the review and leaves the decision to her? What validation level does she require?
- Baseline: which metric, perimeter and data source, signed by the controller in week 1?
- Where is the real queue in the 18 days? Measure each step: packaging, review, lab, deviation, QP.
- Who pays for validation, and who owns it on the CIO side?
- How do we restate the €9M to the board without the CFO losing face?
